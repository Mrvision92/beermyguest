Beer getaway: a pilgrimage to Hop Country and Westvleteren

20 August 2021, by Beer My Guest

Savour the brewing culture of Hoppeland in Belgium

Do you love relaxing weekends filled with craft experiences, little brewing discoveries and good local food? Then it’s simple: you need to spend a weekend in Hoppeland (Hop Country) in West Flanders! The region is beautiful and perfect for lovely walks. On foot or by bike, go and admire the hop fields and the fine breweries, and enjoy the charming holiday cottages… We go there every year to recharge our batteries and collect our beers from Westvleteren Abbey! It’s our annual beer pilgrimage.

You can visit the region all year round, but bear in mind that the hop gardens are at their tallest and most splendid in August and early September.

There is a huge number of walks and visits to enjoy in the area. The town of Poperinge offers several routes and ideas on its website.

Three activities you simply can’t miss!

  1. A visit to the ‘T Hoppecruyt hop farm
  2. Collecting your beers at Westvleteren Abbey (plan well ahead!)
  3. A tour and tasting at the Sint-Bernardus Brewery

1. The 'T Hoppecruyt hop farm

Hops have been grown in Belgium for centuries. But… Belgium’s hop farms very nearly disappeared altogether in favour of the big groups. Let’s face it: the overwhelming majority of the hops used in our Belgian beers today come from abroad (the biggest producers being the United States, Germany and the Czech Republic).

Even so, a few small, indomitable hop farms in Poperinge have held out! Among them is ‘T Hoppecruyt, a local, family-run, eco-friendly hop farm. The visit is well worth the trip. Over 1h30, the cheerful and friendly owner Benedikte Coutigny takes you around her farm, full of authentic charm! She reveals all the secrets of her work, opens the doors of her sheds and lets you taste the local beer brewed with their own hops: the “Hommel bier”.

The best moment of the visit, the one that will stay with us forever, was when Bénédickte brought out her accordion and sang us a song from her region in the local dialect! A wonderful exchange with passionate local producers. An authentic experience, everything we love! Thank you, Bénédikte!

Visits cost around €8 per person. For any information or bookings, feel free to check their website.

2. Westvleteren and the Abbey of Saint Sixtus

Westvleteren 12… Does it even need an introduction? Nicknamed the Holy Grail of beer, this legendary dark beer, velvety and deep, is brewed under the supervision of the Cistercian monks of the Abbey of Saint Sixtus. The monks have been brewing since 1839 and have kept an incredible know-how. Following the monastic Rule of Saint Benedict, making a profit is not an end in itself. If they sell their beer, it is to support their community and fund good causes. Their motto is crystal clear: “We do not live to brew, we brew to live”.

The abbey produces cheese, jams and 3 kinds of beer: the blond, Westvleteren 8 and Westvleteren 12. It is the latter that has been voted the best beer in the world several times, and it sells for a very high price in Brussels: you can find it in specialist shops for €15 to €18 per 33cl bottle!

This high price comes down to its rarity and how hard it is to get hold of. In theory, barring exceptions, it may not be sold in shops. To get the beer, you have to order online and go directly to the abbey (or, more recently, ask for home delivery). The price is perfectly reasonable, but you can only order 48 bottles per car (not per person!). After that, you have to wait 6 weeks before you can order again.

So yes, it’s complicated to get hold of, but it’s well worth the effort! Driving through the abbey’s “drive-in” to pick up these marvels is undeniably a real pleasure. And even though you can’t visit the monastery itself, this pilgrimage to the abbey is still a wonderful experience. Before or after collecting your order, enjoy a lovely ride or walk in the surrounding area and round it off with a fine beer tasting at the café opposite, In de Vrede.

Insider tip: if you can’t manage to order directly from the abbey, or you want more beers, you can also buy 3 six-packs of Westvleteren at the In de Vrede café shop. Don’t hesitate to pick up their pâté and cheese too… Absolutely to die for!

3. Visit the Sint-Bernardus Brewery

After our trip to Westvleteren, we always visit the Sint-Bernardus Brewery in Watou. This more modern brewery has very close ties with Westvleteren beer. As explained during the tour, its brewers brewed the Westvleteren monks’ beer for decades. But in 1992 the monks decided to take back the brewing and brew within their religious community. The Sint-Bernardus brewers respected that choice… However, they had no wish to close their doors. So they carried on brewing their beer, keeping the recipes inherited from the monks… The beers are therefore very similar and, unlike Westvleteren 12, Sint-Bernardus 12 is very easy to get hold of.

The challenge, of course, is to compare the two beers! Will you be able to tell them apart? It’s harder than it looks! A little tip: Westvleteren 12 tastes slightly sweeter and has a more elegant head. Good luck!

For more information about the tour and the brewery, feel free to visit their website.

Whatever you decide to do in Hoppeland, enjoy your getaway! Cheers!
